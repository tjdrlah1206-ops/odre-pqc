Trust

What runs on your infrastructure, and what doesn't.

Answers to the questions a security review will actually ask, without absolute claims that go past what the product can guarantee.

Does protected traffic pass through ODRE servers?

No. Protected request and response traffic is enforced and stays entirely within your own infrastructure. It is not routed through ODRE servers as part of enforcing the security boundary.

What is the Offline Lease?

Your license is a signed, device-bound lease that the local runtime verifies itself. This is what allows the security boundary to continue enforcing its protections without requiring a continuous, live connection to ODRE's control plane, for the validity period of that lease.

Does the runtime require a login or an account?

No. The runtime itself does not require a login or an account to operate. Account-related activity, where it exists, is limited to purchasing and license management, not to the operation of the security boundary on your servers.

What happens if ODRE's control plane is temporarily unavailable?

A valid, unexpired offline lease allows the local runtime to continue enforcing the security boundary during a control plane outage. Renewal of the lease, and any functionality that specifically depends on a live connection to the control plane, would be affected for the duration of that outage.

What information is handled by the Control Plane?

Purchasing, license issuance, and license/lease management are handled through ODRE's control plane. This is separate from your protected application traffic, which is not part of this relationship.

What stays entirely inside your infrastructure?

All protected request and response traffic, session state, and the enforcement of the security boundary itself run on your own servers.

What is verified locally, on your servers?

Request and response verification, replay and sequence checks, update signature verification, runtime artifact integrity, and lease validation are all performed by the local runtime.

What does Fail-Closed mean in practice?

If a request cannot be verified — whether due to a failed check or an invalid or missing lease — the protected handler does not execute. The boundary is designed to deny by default rather than allow by default when it cannot confirm a request is valid.

What remains your responsibility

ODRE PQC manages the security boundary itself — its own updates, rollback, and recovery. It does not manage the following, which remain part of your own infrastructure and operations:

Application layer

  • Application correctness
  • Authentication & authorization
  • Business logic

Infrastructure layer

  • Database
  • Backups
  • Network configuration
  • Underlying operating system
